Does a Brain Foundation Model Know the Brain's Maps?

I tested TRIBE v2 (Meta/FAIR's video+audio+text → fMRI model) on the classic stimuli neuroscientists use to map the brain, and found a sharp split between what it keeps and what it throws away.
H1 — Retinotopy  ·  Absent
No polar-angle map
The template (left) shows a smooth gradient across visual cortex. TRIBE v2's prediction (right) does not reproduce it — spatial averaging erases position.
d = 0.05, not significant · robust across 7 controls
H3 — Motion  ·  Present
MT+ nails it
Moving vs. static dots produce a sharp response that lands exactly in area MT+ — feature identity survives even though position does not.
d = 4.31, p = 0.001 · 52× enrichment in MT+
H2 — Eccentricity  ·  Looked real, wasn't
An image-statistics illusion
Shrink or grow the ring stimulus (above) and a seemingly real eccentricity gradient appears — it tracks image statistics, not true visual-field position.
flips sign across 3 of 3 geometry controls
H4 — Tonotopy  ·  Absent
No frequency gradient
Best frequency (dots) does not slope across A1's posteromedial-to-anterolateral axis — confirmed even with loudness-equalized tones.
ρ = −0.07, not significant · spread 0.14 octaves
The headline finding
Codes that depend on where something is are destroyed by spatial averaging, in both vision and audition. Codes that depend on what something is — like motion direction — survive intact.
